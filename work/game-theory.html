Case study / algebraic game theory

When games have algebraic structure.

Peer-reviewed publication · Discrete Mathematics Letters · 2025

Our research asks how the structural ideas behind homomorphisms, quotients, and isomorphism theorems extend to impartial combinatorial games.

The idea

Combinatorial games can be represented by directed graphs of positions and legal moves. The paper introduces optiongraphs and option-preserving maps as a framework broad enough to include games with cycles and infinite play.

The mathematical result

With Dana C. Ernst and Nándor Sieben, I coauthored a paper demonstrating that the four isomorphism theorems from universal algebra hold in this category. We studied congruence relations, quotient optiongraphs, and a minimum quotient construction.

The framework preserves important game-theoretic information such as outcomes, remoteness, and extended nim-values under suitable maps.

Why it matters

Abstract algebraic constructions can identify which distinctions between positions matter and which can be compressed away. This can produce smaller game models without losing the relevant information.
