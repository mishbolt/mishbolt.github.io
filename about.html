About

Curiosity is my common thread.

A mathematician with a background in physics, an interest in computation, and a belief that abstract ideas and practical problems make each other more interesting.

I am a PhD student in mathematics at North Carolina State University, based in Raleigh.

My undergraduate training was in both mathematics and physics at Northern Arizona University, where I later earned an M.S. in mathematics. Those experiences shaped the way I approach research: I like formal structure, but I also like seeing mathematical ideas tested against models, data, and computation.

My master's work used topological data analysis and machine learning to recover parameters from patterns generated by a partial differential equation. In another project I studied chaos in a double pendulum. I have also collaborated on published research in the algebraic theory of impartial combinatorial games.

Alongside research, I have spent several years teaching mathematics, including helping establish a Putnam preparation program.

I am especially interested in opportunities involving scientific machine learning, computational mathematics, numerical methods, and mathematical research.
